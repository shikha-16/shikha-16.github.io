The Avenues of a Full Life

by Shikha Bhat · 3 min read

What does it actually mean to live a "full" life? It's a question I've been thinking about, especially as I find myself in new cities, starting over and rebuilding life from the ground up.

This post is deeply personal and might not apply to everyone reading it. These are just some parts of life that I believe matter for fulfillment, and I fully expect to keep updating this list as I live.

1Relationships

We are inherently social creatures. A full life rarely happens in isolation. This avenue isn't about having a massive social circle; it's about the depth and quality of your bonds.

Deep intimacy

Relationships where you can be entirely, unapologetically yourself.

  • Partnership: building a solid, trusting relationship with your significant other.
  • Friendship: a close-knit group of friends who truly know you and will show up for you.
  • Family: staying connected to home and grounded in your roots.

Community

We all need to feel like we are part of something larger than ourselves.

  • Shared interests: joining a local dance class, a hobby group, or a creative subculture.
  • Giving back: volunteering your time to support the people and causes in your neighborhood.

2Purpose

This is about how you leave your mark on the world and how you spend your energy. It's the answer to what wakes you up in the morning.

Meaningful work

Work takes many forms. It can be a traditional career, but it can also be caregiving, building a business from scratch, or creating art.

  • Career: a profession that challenges and fulfills you intellectually, and keeps you occupied and happy.
  • Impact: work that drives positive social change, so you are (or at least feel like you are) working towards something important.
  • Financial stability: having the resources to support yourself and others.

Legacy

Creating, teaching, or supporting things that will outlast you.

  • Family: having kids and raising the next generation.
  • Enduring work: making sure the projects you pour your energy into have a lasting impact.
  • Mentorship: passing down what you've learned and guiding others on their own paths.

3Presence

A full life is felt in the body. It's the move from merely surviving to actively experiencing the world through your senses.

Health

Take care of your physical self so you have the energy to explore, play, and endure.

  • Get outside: breathe fresh air and feel the sun.
  • Stay active: exercise regularly to keep your body strong.
  • Self-care: indulge in the basics. Take long showers, stick to a skincare routine, and nurture your body.
  • Dance: move simply for the sake of moving.

Mindfulness

The ability to slow down. Actually tasting your food, noticing a sunset, or being fully present in a conversation rather than constantly projecting into the future or the past.

  • Mental health: actively take care of your mind.
  • Rest: build meditation, stillness, and proper rest into your routine.
  • Self-reflection: regularly check in with yourself to see how you're truly feeling.

4Growth

A life feels small when it becomes entirely predictable. Growth keeps the mind sharp and the spirit adaptable. Autonomy keeps you free.

Lifelong learning

Never lose the desire to pursue curiosity for its own sake.

  • Deep dives: do your own research on topics that fascinate you.
  • Side goals: always have something new you're trying to master, whether it's a language, an instrument, or reading deeply about a topic.

Self-sovereignty

The psychological freedom to make choices aligned with your own values, rather than just reacting to external expectations or seeking approval.

  • Independent thought: spend time actively thinking about your life, your goals, and what you actually want out of all of it.

5Whimsy

A full life isn't just a list of responsibilities and achievements; it needs texture, color, and play.

Unstructured play

Do things entirely for the joy of doing them, with no optimization, performance metrics, or financial return in mind.

  • Embrace whimsy: allow yourself to be silly and spontaneous.
  • Explore: travel and seek out new experiences.
  • Treat yourself: spend money on things that simply bring you joy.

Creative expression

Give form to your inner world through art, writing, cooking, gardening, or problem-solving.

  • Immerse in art: play music, draw, or lose yourself in a great book.

A final thought

A full life doesn't mean having all five figured out perfectly at the same time. Different seasons of life call for prioritizing different avenues.

Be gentle with yourself. Focus on the avenue that is calling to you right now, and trust that the others will be there when you're ready for them.
