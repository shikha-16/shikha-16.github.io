The Generation Google Scholarship

by Shikha Bhat · 3 min read

In May 2021, I received the Generation Google Scholarship APAC: for women in computer science. Here's my experience, plus some tips if you're applying.

What is it?

The Generation Google Scholarship helps students pursuing computer science degrees excel in technology and become leaders in the field. Scholars receive $1,000 USD for the upcoming school year. It's awarded based on commitment to diversity, equity and inclusion, demonstrated leadership, and academic performance.

Eligibility (as of 2021)

  • Identify as female.
  • Be enrolled as a full-time student for the coming academic year.
  • Be a first- or second-year Bachelor's student at an accredited university in an eligible Asia Pacific country or region.
  • Study computer science or a closely related technical field.
  • Have a strong academic record.
  • Show leadership and a passion for improving representation of underrepresented groups in tech.

The application process

Applications opened in early March, and I started working on my materials as soon as I heard about it. There were three rounds.

Round 1: Online application

Resume. I made mine in Google Docs and tried to give a complete view of my profile in one page: education, leadership, projects, courses and achievements.

Two essays, assessing leadership and commitment to diversity, equity and inclusion:

  • Give one example of how you have exhibited leadership: what you were trying to achieve, your role, how you influenced others, and the impact.
  • What is a significant challenge that women in the tech industry face, and how do you see yourself as part of the solution?

I wrote about my time as Project Lead at Nirmaan, an NGO on campus. I read up on women in technology and tied the challenges to my own experiences, along with the steps I'd taken and wanted to take. I had friends review the essays, and their feedback helped a lot. We also submitted background information and academic transcripts.

Round 2: Google Online Challenge

Two timed, multiple-choice mini challenges of 30 minutes each: General Cognitive Ability (logical reasoning and problem solving) and Role-Related Knowledge (C, C++, data structures and algorithms, computer fundamentals). I took the practice test and brushed up on DSA. The challenge is just one extra data point in a holistic review.

Round 3: Video conversation

About three weeks later, I was invited to a 30-minute conversation with a Googler, a chance for Google to learn about my interest in the scholarship, my goals, my resume and essays. I was excited and nervous, and prepared answers to likely questions. My interviewer, a Googler in Australia, was really friendly and made me comfortable. We talked about my leadership experiences and my interest in computer science.

Congratulations!

In the last week of May, I got the email: I'd been selected! Scholars receive $1,000, some swag, and an invite to a two-day scholars' retreat hosted by Google (virtual in 2021). The retreat sessions focused on personal growth and building confidence as a woman stepping into tech, and the scholar community is one of the best I've ever been part of.

My advice to applicants

  1. Make it personal. Back up everything with your own experiences or examples of your work.
  2. Get reviews. Have a few friends read your application. They'll help you frame things better and catch what you missed.
  3. Prepare your resume well. Watch videos on crafting resumes, add links wherever you can, and take the prep material seriously.
  4. Show the three things: leadership, community engagement and interest in computer science.
  5. Prepare for the conversation. At least have a short introduction ready so you don't blank out.
  6. Don't self-reject! Fun fact: I didn't get the scholarship when I applied in my first year. I was rejected at the final stage, but I learned so much from the process. Go do your best.
I'm really glad I applied. It helped me gain so much knowledge and confidence, and the friends I made through the community are amazing.

If you have questions about the scholarship, feel free to reach out. All the best!
